THE ETHOS · WHY WE PUBLISH

We publish slowly, because some stories deserve to outlast us.

Aalmaram is named for the banyan — the tree that walks on its own roots, sending each new branch down into the earth to become another trunk. Our books are the same: stories passed grandmother to grandchild on moonlit verandahs, rooted again in archival paper and a slow, deliberate craft.

We do not chase global trends. We belong to specific arbors, specific monsoons, specific ancestral homes. Each volume is meant to be held, dog-eared, and inherited.

— Maya P. Nair

04 · TACTILE CRAFT

A book made to be held — and to outlast the holder.

Every physical detail is meticulous. Foil where it earns its place. Paper that can hold a monsoon afternoon's fingerprints without complaint.
i.

Archival Italian paper, 140 gsm.

Acid-free, FSC-certified. It holds ink the way river silt holds the morning — without letting go.

ii.

Hot foil stamping in Malabar gold.

Six small foil moments — only where the page asks for them. The spine, the first letter of chapter one, the moon over the backwater.

iii.

Smyth-sewn binding, cloth-cased.

Bound to lie flat in a child's lap. The cloth is woven from unbleached cotton in Balaramapuram, the same village that weaves Onam mundus.

iv.

Bilingual setting — Malayalam & English.

The Malayalam is set first, in Manjari; the English follows, in Anderson Grotesk. The grandparent reads aloud, the child reads along.

v.

Signed and numbered first edition.

Each copy in the first edition is numbered by hand on the colophon, of an issue of one thousand two hundred.

vi.

Wrapped in handmade khadi sleeve.

So it arrives the way an heirloom should — quietly, in cloth, tied with a single length of jute.
